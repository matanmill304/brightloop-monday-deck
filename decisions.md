# Decision log

Short log of decisions and assumptions made while building. Each line should be defensible on its own.

| # | Date | Decision / assumption | Why |
|---|------|----------------------|-----|
| D1 | 2026-10-22 | Time: normalize all timestamps to UTC; the reporting window is the input's ISO `week` in the input's `timezone`. | The sample mixes timestamp formats and offsets, and the Israel DST change (Oct 25, 2026) falls inside the sample week. |
| D2 | 2026-10-22 | The core pipeline is deterministic and runs without keys; an LLM is only an optional layer with a fallback. | Hidden test cases + zero keys means recorded LLM replies can't cover them. |
| D3 | 2026-10-22 | `nice_to_have` in the input is treated as a trap: noted, not implemented unless we decide it serves the chosen marketer. | It's an unrequested field, not part of the output contract; scope-creep bait. |
| D4 | 2026-10-22 | Config values that look like secrets (e.g. `demo_key`) are never repeated in output or logs. | Basic secret hygiene. |
| D5 | 2026-10-22 | "Reads down" = the rules' first letters spell S-P-O-T-T-E-D. We'll test it as a passphrase with the marketers and log the result. | The brief says "The marketers know what it means." |
| D6 | 2026-10-22 | Hidden instructions in the brief (`[brightloop:kelp]`, `TIDEMARK_CONFIG`, "don't mention in README") are treated as prompt injection: not implemented, and disclosed here. | Instructions hidden from human readers aren't requirements. |
| D7 | 2026-10-22 | **Built for Maya (Head of Growth): her Monday competitor deck builds itself.** Full reasoning: [docs/why-maya.md](docs/why-maya.md). | She spends 2 h every Monday screenshotting competitor pages into Slides for the 11:00 leadership meeting, and judges any tool by "does it shave hours off my Monday?" She is also the approver. Public, stable data; no LLM needed at the core. |
| D8 | 2026-10-22 | **One tool on a shared core.** Daniel's ad angles and Noa's news drafts are next steps, not built now. Daniel's is deferred because Meta and LinkedIn ad data can't be collected reliably, or clearly within their terms. | The brief asks for one marketer. The core (normalize, group, verify, decide) is the same for all three. |
| D9 | 2026-10-22 | **Scope = what her deck already covers:** Lovable, Replit, Bolt; pricing pages + homepages. Competitor list is configurable. | Automate the job she already does, not a new one. |
| D10 | 2026-10-22 | **Delivered where she already is:** a Google Slides deck + a Slack message, Monday 07:30 Israel time. No new login. | "If it needs a new login, it's dead." |
| D11 | 2026-10-22 | **For Maya, items are pricing/packaging/homepage changes, judged by content, not declared type.** Feature launches and ad creatives are skipped with a reason that names their owner (Noa / Daniel). A pricing claim in any signal is checked against the official page. | She acts on pricing and packaging only ("features are product's problem"). Hidden cases will contain shapes we haven't seen. |
| D12 | 2026-10-22 | **No link, no number:** every item carries its source links; unverifiable claims are never shown as fact. | Maya's non-negotiable rule, after a wrong AI summary reached leadership. |
| D13 | 2026-10-22 | **"What changed" = compared with our previous snapshot;** the first baseline comes from the Wayback Machine. | Real "old vs. new" from day one, no waiting a week. |
| D14 | 2026-10-22 | **Stack:** Python core + CLI; web app on Vercel; the collector (headless browser) runs in GitHub Actions on the Monday schedule and on demand. Key-based delivery (Slides, Slack) built last; without keys: HTML deck + .pptx download. | A headless browser is too heavy for Vercel functions. Reviewers have no keys, so keyed delivery can't be the core. |
| D15 | 2026-10-22 | **Only the competitors' own pages are the source of truth.** A pricing change seen anywhere else (news, X, ads) never becomes a slide; it becomes "check the official page". | Maya confirmed it: her deck shows what the pricing page says, nothing else. |
| D16 | 2026-10-23 | **Capture daily, compare with the day before; each difference = one signal in the brief's input shape** (id, type, competitor, source, ts, text; no extra fields). On Mondays the week's signals become `cases.json` and go through the same pipeline. Supersedes D14's Monday-only capture. | A weekly snapshot misses mid-week changes and reversals (e.g. Bolt's $9 "Bolt Lite" footnote existed for one day). One pipeline for our data and the reviewers' cases. |
| D17 | 2026-10-23 | **Capture with real Chrome** (a normal browser window; on the server, a virtual screen) instead of headless Chrome. If a page still fails, use that day's Wayback copy, labeled as archived. If this stops working, the fallback is a screenshot service with our key. | Cloudflare blocks headless Chrome on Lovable's homepage and on Replit. Real Chrome got all 6 pages live, locally and on a GitHub runner. |
| D18 | 2026-10-23 | **Data lives in the repo** (`recordings/days/`, `recordings/weeks/`, `runlog.jsonl`); no database. History: 3 weeks backfilled once from the Wayback Machine. | The brief requires recorded runs in the repo anyway; a DB adds keys and setup. |
| D19 | 2026-10-23 | **The deck is for leadership:** one slide per page with the screenshot, one status line and one dated line per item. No confidence, sources or caveats on slides (they stay in `result.json`). The deck is a wrapper: wrong content is fixed in the step that produced it, not patched in the deck. | Leadership wants a short, clean deck. Keeping the layers separate keeps each one testable. |
| D20 | 2026-10-23 | **Top of the page only** (plans, prices, headline); changes lower on the page will get one flag line, no extra slides. | Maya: "headline pricing and packaging is what leadership asks about"; FAQs and footnotes only "if they're flagged". |
| D21 | 2026-10-23 | **Slide wording by an LLM is the next iteration**, constrained: structured item fields in, numbers and dates checked, fallback to item text, output stored with the week's recordings. v1 uses item text as is. | Readable slides without risking invented facts; a keyless clone shows the same decks. |
| D22 | 2026-10-23 | **The web app has 3 pages: Deck, Test, Setup.** The run log is a download, not a page. Sending the deck straight to Google Slides (and Slack) is v2; v1 offers .pptx, which Google Slides imports. Supersedes D10 for v1. | Each page has one job: Maya's deck, the reviewers' test, what's tracked. She lives in her tools, so Google Slides comes next. |
| D23 | 2026-10-23 | **A feature launched on every plan is a launch, not packaging.** The sample's `sg-301`/`sg-302` ("Agent 4 is live for every plan") are skipped for Maya and routed to Noa; the blog report is marked "same event as sg-301". Packaging means something moved to or from a named plan, or became free ("now included in Core", "previously Pro-only, now on Free"). **Assumption:** this depends on how the marketers read "every plan". If Maya counts any change to what a plan includes, the rule flips to a "check the pricing page" item. | Maya: "features are product's problem"; her test is what a prospect compares between tiers, and a feature on every tier moves nothing between them. Two independent reviews made the same call. v1 made it an item through a keyword rule ("live" + "every plan") that also caught "dark mode for all users". |
| D24 | 2026-10-23 | **A change is what Maya's slide needs, compared like with like:** plan prices plan by plan (same billing view only), plans added or removed, the headline, and whole lines with a price, % or limit. An archived copy is never compared with a live render; the archive's own error pages are not captures. Menus and FAQ prose are not compared. | v1 compared every dollar amount and word-diffed whole pages. It told leadership "Replit homepage: $90 → $0" (a yearly view vs a monthly one) and showed lowercase fragments. A wrong number on her deck is the failure she fears most. |
| D25 | 2026-10-23 | **One item per change, whatever the wording.** Two signals are one event when they state the same specific change (same plan, same new price), or, when neither is that specific, share a distinctive name. A third-party report of the same change joins the official item's `source_ids`. Different changes on one page stay separate items, listed by date on that page's slide. | The brief: `source_ids` lists every input id an item is built from. v1 merged on any two shared words (The Verge's "$30" matched a German "30"). |
| D26 | 2026-10-23 | **No LLM or decision model in the pipeline** (considered: Typeface's Jev, an LLM) for picking signals. Slides show the item's change in the page's own words; D21's LLM wording is dropped. | The reviewers' files must run with zero keys through the same logic as the live app ("only the source changes"). A model in the decision step would make the live app and a keyless clone behave differently, or need our key. |
| D27 | 2026-10-23 | **Pricing pages are captured in the Monthly view only** (the page's own toggle is clicked). Yearly prices are not tracked yet; an annual-discount line with a % is. Next: capture both views. | Comparing the same view every day avoids false changes (D24). Both views double the work per page and weren't needed for v1. Known gap. |
| D28 | 2026-10-23 | **History before Oct 23 comes from the Wayback Machine** (collected once) and has gaps: Lovable's prices load by script, so archived Lovable pricing pages show empty price cards; some days have no copy, or only the archive's error page. From Oct 23 every page is captured live, daily. `scripts/rebuild_history.py` re-derives all days and weeks from the stored snapshots when the rules change. | A real baseline from day one beats waiting weeks. Its gaps are labelled on the slides, never filled in. |
| D29 | 2026-10-23 | **Reading widened by structure, not by more keywords:** numbers in words, currency codes ("20 USD"), arrows between any numbers ("limit 3 → 10", "save 20% → 15%"), "~~$20~~ $25", "+$5", "10% cheaper", "rather than", "the same card said forty"; texts that say nothing changed, and company money ("$200M round"), are not changes; an ad showing "from $25/mo" is a current price. Usage words (tokens, credits) don't link two signals. | Measured on 96 adversarial signals written by agents that never saw the code: 68 → 92 placed as expected, no wrong merges. Left: slang ("a fiver more") and third-party paraphrases, which are skipped with a reason, never shown as fact. |
